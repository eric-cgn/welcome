Etiquette

Most of the places in this guide are open to anyone, and many are kept by people who pay for them out of their own pockets. Being a good guest helps keep them open. Etiquette on the Internet is mostly the courtesy you would show anywhere, with a few customs of its own.

Ask for what you need

Every request you send uses a little of a server's time, and a little of the connection someone pays for. Ask for what you want, and avoid asking for the same thing over and over. If a page is slow to arrive, give it time before you try again.

Some servers can serve only so many visitors at once. If one tells you it is busy, or doesn't answer, try again later.

Read the notes

Many servers greet you with a welcome message, or offer a file with a name like README, the way a program comes with a Read Me document. These explain what the place offers and any rules its owners ask you to follow. Read them first.

Newsgroups

Newsgroups have customs of their own, which you can read about in Newsgroups. The first is to read a group for a while before you write to it. Many groups have a list of answers to frequently asked questions, called a FAQ, and it is polite to read it before you ask.

Mill reads newsgroups but doesn't post to them. If you write to a newsgroup with another program, remember that it can be read by many people you don't know.

Important

Mill sends what you type, and servers send what you ask for, as plain text. As you read in Rules of the Road, your request is carried by a relay of computers, and the people who run any of them could read it.

When a server asks for a password, Mill usually sends it as plain text too. Use a password you don't use anywhere else, and don't send anything you would mind a stranger reading.

A proxy, described in The World Wide Web, can reach sites that use encryption for you, but the leg between your Macintosh and the proxy is still plain text.

Saying thank you

The people who keep these places running do it so that others can visit. Visiting, reading their notes, and thanking them when you can are good ways to keep those places going.
