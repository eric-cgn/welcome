Your Macintosh

Most addresses lead to a server somewhere else. Two kinds lead somewhere much closer: to the documents on your own Macintosh, and to pages built into Mill itself. These are the addresses that, as you read in Addresses, work a little differently.

Try this

Type file:/// (the word file, a colon, and three slashes).

Mill shows a page called Disks, with each disk on your desktop. Click a disk's name to see what's in it, much as you would in the Finder. Click Up to go back one level.

Documents on your Macintosh

An address that begins with file leads to a document, folder, or disk on your own Macintosh. It has no server's name, because nothing needs to be asked of another computer. That's why it has three slashes in a row: the two that come before a server's name, with nothing between them and the third.

After the slashes come the disk, then each folder, then the document, separated by slashes, the same way you'd open them one after another in the Finder. A document named Thank You, in a folder named Letters, on a disk named Macintosh HD, has this address:

file:///Macintosh%20HD/Letters/Thank%20You

An address can't contain a space, so each space in a name is written %20.

Tip

You don't have to type these addresses. Choose Open File from the File menu (or press Command-O), choose a document the usual way, and Mill opens it and shows its address in the address bar.

Mill shows text documents, Web pages saved on your disk, and pictures in the formats listed in its Settings. For other documents, it tells you it can't show them; open those from the Finder, as usual.

Pages built into Mill

An address that begins with mill leads to a page Mill keeps for itself, built into the application rather than sent by a server. There are four:

mill://about
About Mill: its version, how much memory is free, a button that checks for a newer version, and a list of Mill's keyboard shortcuts. About Mill in the Apple menu opens it too.
mill://config
Settings: your home page, which protocols and formats are turned on, printing, and bookmarks. Preferences in the Edit menu opens it too.
mill://plugins
Plug-Ins: each Plug-In Mill has found and what it does, with a link to its own page when it has one.
mill://license
Licenses: the terms for Mill and for the work of others that it includes.

Why these addresses aren't links

The addresses on this page are written out but not underlined, because a page from the Internet can't open them. Mill keeps pages from the Internet away from your documents and its own pages, so that a page can't reach into your Macintosh without you.

You can reach them in other ways: type them, choose them from a menu, or follow a link from one of your own documents or one of Mill's own pages.
