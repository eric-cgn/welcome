Newsgroups

Try this

Type news://freenews.netfront.net/news.announce.newusers and press Return.

Mill lists the articles in the newsgroup news.announce.newusers, newest first. Click a subject to read the article. This newsgroup carries articles written for people new to newsgroups.

What a newsgroup is

A newsgroup is a public discussion on a single subject. It works much like a bulletin board: people pin up messages, and anyone who comes by can read them. In a newsgroup, a message is called an article, and pinning one up is called posting.

The comparison stops at the board itself, because there is no single board. Each news server keeps its own copy of the newsgroups it carries, and news servers pass new articles along to one another, so an article posted at one server finds its way to many others. Together, the newsgroups and the servers that pass them along are called Usenet.

Reading a newsgroup's name

A newsgroup's name is read from left to right, from the general to the specific. comp.sys.mac.misc is in comp, for computers; then sys, for computer systems; then mac, for the Macintosh; and misc, for miscellaneous discussion that doesn't fit a narrower group.

Other names begin with rec for recreation, sci for science, and news for Usenet itself.

A little history

Usenet began in 1980, when graduate students connected computers at Duke University and the University of North Carolina to share messages. Mill reads newsgroups with the Network News Transfer Protocol, or NNTP. Its rulebook is RFC 977, published in 1986.

Choosing a news server

To read newsgroups, you need a news server. If your Internet provider has one, it can tell you the server's name. Many news servers ask you to sign up for an account. When a server asks for a name and password, Mill asks you for them. NNTP sends them as plain text, so use a password you don't use anywhere else.

Some news servers let anyone read without an account. freenews.netfront.net, the server in Try this, is one of them, and it is offered to readers at no charge.

Newsgroup addresses

In Mill, every newsgroup address names its server:

  • news://freenews.netfront.net/ lists every newsgroup the server carries, with about how many articles each has. To find groups, type part of a name, such as mac, in the Find groups box and click Find.
  • news://freenews.netfront.net/comp.sys.mac.misc lists a newsgroup's articles, newest first. Older articles and Newer articles, at the bottom, take you through the rest.
  • news://freenews.netfront.net/comp.sys.mac.misc/number shows one article by its number in the newsgroup.

Addresses that begin with nntp:// work the same way.

Good manners

Read a newsgroup for a while before you post to it, so you learn what its readers talk about. Many newsgroups have a FAQ, a list of frequently asked questions with their answers, posted regularly. Look for it before you ask a question; the answer may be waiting.

What Mill needs

Mill reads newsgroups with its News Plug-In. To see whether it is installed, type mill://plugins and look for News.

While specialized programs called newsreaders can also post articles, Mill is made for reading: it lists a server's newsgroups and finds them by name, shows each group's articles a page at a time, and shows each article's text. Pictures and other files attached to an article are named, not shown.

If you read news from one server most of the time, you can save its name:

  1. Choose Preferences from the Edit menu. Mill opens its Settings page.
  2. Under Protocols, find News, and type the server's name in Default server.
  3. Click Save, at the bottom of the page.

Then plugin://news/about has a form with the server filled in: type a newsgroup's name and click Open. Under News, Articles per page sets how many articles each page lists.
